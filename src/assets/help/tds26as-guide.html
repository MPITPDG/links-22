26AS TDS Reconciliation

This screen compares the TDS your customers have deposited against your PAN (as reported in the Income-tax Form 26AS) with what you actually billed them in the ERP — customer by customer — so you can claim the full TDS credit in the income-tax return and chase what's missing.

Where to find it

Accounts → Master → 26AS TDS Reconcil. The same screen handles upload and the report. When you open it, the last uploaded statement loads automatically — you only upload again when a fresh 26AS is downloaded.

How to use it — step by step

  1. Download Form 26AS from the income-tax portal

    Log in to the e-filing portal → e-File → Income Tax Returns → View Form 26AS (TRACES). Choose the financial year and download as Text — the file looks like AAACM3908L-2026-200230242.txt. Upload it as-is; an Excel (.xlsx) copy of the same statement also works.

  2. Upload the file

    Click Upload New Statement, pick the file, then Upload & Reconcile. The system reads PART-I (TDS deducted by customers), matches each deductor to your client master, and opens the report. Re-uploading the same year's statement simply replaces the old one — nothing is duplicated.

  3. Map the unmatched customers (one-time)

    Open the Unmatched tab. Where the customer's 26AS name differs from your ledger name, pick the right client from the dropdown and press Save. The mapping is remembered by TAN, so next year's upload matches automatically.

  4. Review the differences

    Work down the Difference column (see reading guide below). Click the number in the Txns column to see the customer's individual 26AS entries side by side with your invoices for the year.

  5. Export for working papers

    Export downloads the current view (whichever tab is open) as an Excel file with totals — ready to hand to the auditor or attach to the ITR working file.

What the Difference column means

ReadingTypical causeWhat to do
0.00 Customer booked exactly what you billed. (e.g. AMI Pigments — 1,66,003 both sides.) Nothing — credit is claimable as-is.
Positive (amber) You invoiced more than they reported. Usually timing: your March bills booked by them in April (next FY), or they deduct TDS only on your service charges, not on reimbursements/duty. Drill down and compare dates. A March invoice appearing in next year's 26AS is normal; only chase the customer if TDS itself looks short.
Negative (red) They reported more than you invoiced this FY. Usually last year's bills booked by them this year, or an advance. Verify the income is really yours before treating the credit as claimable. Prior-year invoices carrying this year's TDS may need Form 71 (see below).
Blank (—) Row not mapped to a client yet, so there is nothing to compare. Map it from the dropdown and Save.

Timing differences are normal, not errors. 26AS follows the customer's booking date, your ERP follows your invoice date. A bill raised on 31 March and booked by the customer on 5 April will always show as a difference in both years — it offsets across the two statements.

TDS in this year's 26AS for income you billed last year? Credit can still be recovered by filing Form 71 on the e-filing portal (allowed up to two years from the end of the FY in which the TDS was deducted — for FY 2025-26 deductions, until 31 March 2028). Flag such rows for your CA.

A deductor in 26AS you don't recognise at all? Don't claim that credit blindly — it may be someone reporting against the wrong PAN. Confirm the income is yours first; otherwise the deductor must file a correction on TRACES.

The drill-down (click the Txns number)

The left panel lists every 26AS entry for that customer — section (194C etc.), transaction date, booking status, amount and TDS. The right panel lists your invoices to that client for the year. Use it to tick entries off against invoices and to spot exactly which bills the customer has or hasn't booked.

In the Status column of 26AS entries, F (Final) means the deductor's payment is matched with the bank and the credit is secure. U (Unmatched) or P (Provisional) entries should be re-checked in a later 26AS download before relying on them.

The three totals at the top

  • 26AS Amount Credited — everything customers reported paying you in the FY.
  • 26AS TDS Deposited — the total TDS credit available against your PAN. This is the figure to reconcile with the TDS receivable in your books and claim in the ITR.
  • FY Invoiced (matched clients) — your billing to the matched customers. It grows as you map more rows, so map everything before comparing grand totals.

Month-end / year-end routine

  1. Download a fresh 26AS quarterly

    Deductors file quarterly, and entries keep arriving for up to two months after each quarter ends. The year is only complete around end-May of the next FY.

  2. Upload, then clear the Unmatched tab to zero

    Every saved mapping is remembered — this gets faster each quarter.

  3. Investigate red (negative) rows first

    They affect what credit you may claim. Amber rows are mostly timing and usually explain themselves in the drill-down.

  4. Export and file the reconciliation

    Keep the export with the TDS receivable schedule for the auditor and the return.
